# Membership Strategy Discussion: Nathan Network

I think the strategy is sound, but the current labels and entitlement model need tightening before the membership page explains it. The key is to sell outcomes, not seven separate technical modules.

Your offer has a credible commercial structure:

| Component | Monthly value |
|---|---:|
| Intelligence | R100 |
| Transaction capability | R300 |
| Loyalty | R100 |
| Commercial pathway opt-ins: 7 malls at R100 each | R700 |
| **Total standalone value** | **R1,200** |
| **Nathan network all-in offer** | **R500** |
| Additional business role | R250/month per role |

The additional business role fee is separate from the R1,200 standalone membership value because it expands a company into another operating portal, such as Supplier, Transporter, or Finance. It is optional and applies when a member wants to operate in an additional role beyond their existing business role.

The all-in R500 proposition is compelling because it reduces decision fatigue: a member does not need to predict whether they will require transport, storage, funding, suppliers, loads, and so on. That is particularly valuable in logistics, where one operational need often creates another.

The pay-as-you-go model still matters, but I would position it as a **controlled entry route**, not as an equal alternative to the bundle. If both options are presented with equal emphasis, members may choose the cheapest item and never experience enough value to remain engaged.

## Recommended Membership Architecture

Keep the conceptual tiers, but make each one serve a distinct decision:

| Plan | Purpose | Suggested contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic** | Explore the ecosystem | Account, company profile, limited directory discovery, loyalty visibility, no operational commercial access |
| **Standard: Pay as You Go** | Buy only what is needed | Intelligence R100, Transaction R300, Loyalty R100, then individual mall pathways at R100 per mall |
| **Premium: All-In Network Membership** | Run commercial activity across the network | Intelligence, transaction tools, loyalty, and all seven mall pathways |
| **Nathan Network Offer** | Partner-specific Premium | Same Premium entitlement, R500/month instead of R1,200/month |

An additional business role can be activated at R250/month per role. This is distinct from commercial pathway access: a pathway lets the business use a mall; an additional role lets the company operate a different business portal and publish the capabilities associated with that role.

I would avoid calling the R500 offer “Premium” if you intend to sell a higher enterprise product later. Better names:

- `Network Access` for Basic
- `Flexible Access` for pay-as-you-go
- `All-In Membership` for the R1,200 standard bundle
- `Nathan Network All-In Offer` for the R500 partner rate

That leaves room for a future `Enterprise` tier with multiple staff seats, enhanced intelligence, workflow controls, API integrations, reporting, and priority support.

## How the Membership Page Should Be Arranged

The first decision should be a simple choice between two columns, with the all-in option visually dominant:

1. **All-In Membership**
   - “Everything required to find, transact, earn loyalty value, and operate across the Logistics Flow network.”
   - Show the R1,200 value breakdown.
   - Show `R500/month` for eligible Nathan-network members.
   - Include all seven commercial pathways as named badges or a compact list.
   - Use a strong primary action: `Activate All-In Membership`.

2. **Flexible Pay As You Go**
   - “Start with only the services you need and add pathways as your business grows.”
   - Show the base services and each mall add-on clearly:
     - Intelligence: R100
     - Transaction: R300
     - Loyalty: R100
     - Each commercial pathway: R100/month
     - Each additional business role: R250/month
   - Include a running monthly estimate based on selected items.
   - State plainly that the all-in membership becomes cheaper once a member selects enough components.

The pricing comparison needs one important device: a **break-even message**. For example:

> Selecting Intelligence, Transaction, Loyalty and two commercial pathways costs R700/month. The All-In Membership gives all seven pathways for R500/month through the Nathan Network Offer.

Additional business roles should be shown as an optional expansion after membership selection, rather than included in the all-in comparison. For example:

> Need to operate in another portal? Add a Supplier, Transporter, or Finance role for R250/month per additional role.

That makes the bundle economically self-evident without relying on marketing language.

## The Important Product Correction

“Mall access” should not be a binary sidebar permission alone. It should have three states:

1. **Discover**: the member can open the mall workspace and understand its purpose.
2. **Opt in**: the member can activate that commercial pathway for R100/month, or receive it through All-In membership.
3. **Operate**: the member can use protected search, publishing, hub, transaction, and lead functions according to their subscription and role.

This resolves the issue encountered with Loads Mall. A transporter should be able to open the Loads Mall workspace and see:

- what matching freight to fleet capacity does;
- why it benefits their utilisation and revenue;
- whether their current membership includes it;
- an `Activate Loads Pathway` action when it does not;
- or direct operational cards when it does.

The platform should not hide the commercial opportunity before explaining it.

One further distinction should be clear in the product: an additional role is not the same as access to a commercial pathway. A transporter can opt into the Loads Mall pathway to find and match loads. The same company would add an additional Supplier, Finance, or other business role only when it needs to operate and publish in that separate portal.

## One Challenge to the R1,200 Value Claim

The R1,200 total is arithmetically correct, but it will only feel credible if members can see independent utility in each R100 mall pathway. That requires each mall workspace to clearly show:

- the commercial problem it solves;
- what data is used to match members;
- the result the member can expect;
- what actions become available after opt-in.

Without that, “seven malls at R100 each” can feel like seven navigation labels rather than seven purchasable commercial services.

My recommendation is therefore: make **All-In Membership** the default strategic product; retain **Flexible Pay As You Go** as a lower-commitment entry and spend-control mechanism; treat additional business roles as an optional R250/month expansion; and redesign pathway permissions around `Discover → Opt in → Operate` rather than simply allowing or blocking the whole mall.
